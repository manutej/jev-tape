Low confidence is not ask again. It is amber.

After the legend fix, the depth experiment measured four runs across ten depths: strict accuracy and item recall at each. This demo sends those numbers to the judge as one Choice per depth, GREEN / AMBER / RED, and routes the answers with two dials. When the pill is green the answers are recorded from one real call; otherwise a twin builds them from the accuracies, labelled as a device. θ is the threshold on P(GREEN). The floor is how peaked the answer must be. A depth that falls short is not asked again. It goes to review.

What the tape shows

One request carries ten Choice questions, route_D1 through route_D10, over one state: the run's per-depth strict accuracy and item recall. The answer map is written to the tape once, with one POST. Everything after that is code.

composeAnswers turns each answer into a route. A GREEN choice with P(GREEN) under θ becomes review. Any answer whose top probability sits under the floor becomes review, whatever it chose. RED would block. Drag the dials after a run and watch the tape: a code cell lands, the dots recolour, and the POST counter does not move. Low confidence is not a reason to ask the judge again.

Counterpoint

Accuracy is not belief. P(GREEN) here is a measured frequency from one run on one corpus, not the judge's confidence about one question. Using it as the answer's probability is a demo device, and the chart says so. Depth 6 is worse than that: its gold is uniform and name-derivable, so its 1.0 measures nothing. It is marked ⊘ and should not count as evidence.
